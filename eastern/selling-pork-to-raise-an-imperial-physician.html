At first there was Cui Taohua's giggling behind the tree, which gradually turned into pained moaning, then cursing, then screams of agony. Everyone trembled, faces white. "I'll kill these beasts!" Da Niang could bear it no longer and ran out with the knife. "No, Jiang Dasao, those are officers!" "We don't dare touch them..." "If we don't kill them, they won't spare us either." Someone whispered in despair. Right: once evil spirits enter a village, how could only one person be harmed? Da Niang killed the three soldiers. When Da Niang came out carrying Cui Taohua on the back from behind the tree, both looked as though they had stepped out of a pool of blood. Madam Lin fled in terror and vanished. A deep gash on Da Niang's face had laid the flesh bare. Cui Taohua's clothes were torn to shreds, the skin bared and bloody. Everyone rushed over at once. "My girl, you've suffered." The woman who sold vegetables, eyes brimming, took off a cloak and draped it over Cui Taohua. No one hurled any more sneers or curses at Cui Taohua over a naked body. Cui Taohua clung to Da Niang's back, face bruised black and blue, yet a smile bloomed. Miss Jiang bit down to suppress the trembling and carefully bandaged Da Niang's wound. With mulberry silk thread, Miss Jiang sewed, stitch by stitch, the long gash across Da Niang's face. Now Da Niang looked even fiercer, but no one feared Da Niang any longer. Renren and Cui Taohua both wished to be near Da Niang, and the other children of the meat market gathered round too. Crowding together, the children whispered about the stitches on Da Niang's face, saying they looked just like a feather. "We have killed Prince Cheng's soldiers. Sooner or later they'll find out and come for revenge." Blacksmith Zhang gathered everyone and looked around at the children of the families. "We older folk have no fear of dying, but our children cannot die with us..." After discussion, everyone agreed that Da Niang would lead the women and children up North Mountain to hide, while the men remained in town to take Prince Cheng's wrath head-on. Da Niang said not a word. That very night, Da Niang and a few aunties from the village packed some dry rations, tucked the hog knife into the belt, and led the children out of the meat market. "I'm not going! I'm grown, not a child anymore. I want to stay and fight alongside Father and everyone!" Shitou flatly refused to go. Blacksmith Zhang, furious, gave Shitou a hard kick: "What can you do by staying except give your life? Get out!" "Who says I'll give my life? I will kill a few enemy soldiers before I die!" Shitou answered back stubbornly. Not until Blacksmith Zhang was angry to the point of tears did Shitou reluctantly follow the group. After going up the mountain and settling in temporarily, Miss Jiang asked the blind master to keep tending Da Niang's wound. Noticing Shitou's glance, the two understood one another silently and slipped back toward town. The meat market was deserted now, with only corpses sprawled about. Miss Jiang and Shitou wept as they tidied up and carefully buried each one. "Father, Mother, I will surely avenge you all!" Shitou knelt before the grave and knocked the head hard against the ground four times. In the days after, Miss Jiang and Shitou disguised as beggar children and wandered the town. Each time a lone soldier appeared, Shitou knocked the man out with a staff at once, and Miss Jiang drove a silver needle straight into the man's head, killing without a sound. No one imagined that while the whole city trembled and begged for mercy, two bold children were silently hunting soldiers. At the same time, the court's army entered the city. The troops fought fiercely with Prince Cheng's forces. Miss Jiang and Shitou were overjoyed. Shitou promptly asked to join the army, while Miss Jiang followed behind to care for the wounded. Unexpectedly, in the ranks Miss Jiang met an acquaintance again, Young Master Lin. It turned out Young Master Lin had pretended to surrender to Prince Cheng, then seized an opportunity to slip out and ask the court for troops. Young Master Lin, eyes red, called out "Miss Jiang," but Miss Jiang did not answer. Pretending not to know the man, Miss Jiang quietly bowed over to keep bandaging the soldiers' wounds. Before long, Prince Cheng and the remnant forces were wiped out entirely. The County Commandant who had invited bandits into the house was sentenced to death by slicing. Young Master Lin, credited with going out to seek aid, became a hero in the eyes of all the people in the city. Every household praised Young Master Lin, with only the survivors of the meat market keeping silent. Young Master Lin was a benefactor of the whole city, but Madam Lin was an unforgivable enemy of Miss Jiang and the market folk! Word was that since fleeing the meat market in terror, Madam Lin had hidden here and there. On the road Madam Lin met thugs again, was robbed of everything, and struck on the head until falling into a coma. Young Master Lin sought out every physician to treat the mother, all in vain, and Madam Lin's condition worsened by the day. "Miss Jiang, please save my mother!" Who would have thought that the day would come when Young Master Lin would seek out Miss Jiang to beg. The face was gaunt, the eyes full of pleading. "Whatever mistakes my mother once made, I am willing to bear all of it in her place. I only ask that you take pity and save her life!" Young Master Lin dragged the unconscious Madam Lin before Miss Jiang's door. The surviving neighbors had all gathered before the door, silently watching. On every face was etched suppressed hatred. "You'll repay for her? How will you repay?" Shitou, knife pointed straight at Young Master Lin, the innocent face of former days gone, replaced by savagery. "She owes us seventeen lives of the meat market. What do you have to repay with!" Shitou roared. Young Master Lin bowed very low but still refused to give up: "But a physician must have benevolence. How can one watch someone die and not save them!" Young Master Lin used the logic of medicine to coerce Miss Jiang. "I won't save her." Miss Jiang spoke the two words slowly, not bothering to explain or defend. Call Miss Jiang not a good physician if you like. Miss Jiang's cultivation had not reached that far, and generosity enough to save someone bearing deep hatred was beyond reach. In this world, who doesn't suffer? Miss Jiang was no Bodhisattva delivering all beings, only an ordinary girl abandoned by parents from childhood and raised by the kindly people of this meat market. When Madam Lin drew the last breath, not one person came to the Lin house to pay respects. Everyone gathered at the graves of loved ones and wept to the heart's content. Miss Jiang, Shitou, Renren, and Cui Taohua stood gathered beside Da Niang. "Mother!" Shitou, eyes bloodshot, called out chokingly. "Mm! Mm!" Da Niang laughed and wept at once, and the scar on the face seemed to have grown much gentler. "Daughter, this is your eldest brother." Da Niang drew Miss Jiang close. "Big Brother!" Miss Jiang called loudly. "Mm, Little Sister." "I want to take you as my mother too." Renren stepped forward and threw both arms around Da Niang's waist, tears soaking a patch of the jacket. "Good, of course, my good daughter." Renren wiped away tears, turned to look at Miss Jiang, and called sweetly: "Elder Sister!" Miss Jiang patted Renren's head. Renren was the same age as Miss Jiang yet a full head shorter, looking just like a lucky-charm doll. Cui Taohua scratched the head, laughing and crying at once, not daring to speak. "Sister Taohua, you be my mother's daughter too." Renren stepped forward and gripped Cui Taohua's hand. Cui Taohua, hesitant, said softly: "I'd love to, but I'm not worthy..." Before the sentence was finished, Da Niang tapped Cui Taohua's head lightly: "From now on, you are the eldest daughter of this house!" "What?" Cui Taohua's eyes went round with surprise. "But I have one condition. The old trade is not to be practiced anymore. From now on you follow me, slaughtering hogs and selling meat. You are the eldest daughter and must carry on my trade." "Yes!" This time Cui Taohua answered with great decisiveness. Tears poured out without stopping, and Cui Taohua had to keep turning away to wipe them hastily. All of them kowtowed three times before Da Niang, then together prepared a warm reunion meal. The next day, Miss Jiang and Big Brother bade farewell to Da Niang, Big Sister, and Little Sister, packed up, and went on following the army. The master presented Miss Jiang with the medical book that had been treasured all those years. The master also moved back to live in the meat market and rebuilt houses together with the villagers. Time flew like a shuttle, and Miss Jiang's reputation in the army spread ever farther. Before long, Miss Jiang had become a first-rate female physician, always at the General's side to treat the General's wounds, large and small. Five years had flown by since the rebellion of the feudal princes was put down. The General, grateful for having been saved many times, wanted to take Miss Jiang as a concubine. It startled Miss Jiang, who hastily refused. "Why? Do you find it so bitter to follow me?" the General asked, eyes narrowed. "I only fear making the other ladies bitter," Miss Jiang laughed awkwardly and held up the silver needles in hand. "I come from a village and have killed people, and I'm afraid I would be terribly jealous in future." "Hmph!" The General gave a thin laugh, but spoke no more of taking a concubine. The General had personally seen Miss Jiang bring down a prince's soldier with a silver needle when the soldier made a sneak attack, the movement clean and decisive. On the day of the triumphant return to the capital, the General asked Miss Jiang gravely what reward was wanted. "I want to return to my hometown and open a clinic." Miss Jiang thought for a moment, then answered. "You think too little of me!" The General was a little angry. Such a small request was like making light of the debt of a saved life that the General owed. "But I am a woman, and opening a clinic on my own is truly not easy." Miss Jiang explained gently. The General waved a hand and said no more. That same day an imperial edict from the palace was handed down to Miss Jiang. It turned out His Majesty had learned of Miss Jiang's merits in the army and bestowed the title "Female Imperial Physician" together with a gold-plated plaque gleaming brilliantly. Looking at the plaque, Miss Jiang's heart brimmed with joy. This was the surest support for the rest of life! Miss Jiang took leave of the General. The General gave Miss Jiang a cart full of gold and silver, saying the title came from His Majesty while the money was a private reward. Miss Jiang had truly returned home in glory! Everything was going well for Miss Jiang's family. Brother Shitou had won merit on the battlefield and now held the new post of County Commandant. Miss Jiang's mother was respectfully called "Old Madam" wherever Da Niang went. Da Niang always complained that hearing the title raised goosebumps, yet the corners of the mouth could not hold back a beaming smile. Little Sister Renren had married and now had a plump three-year-old child. Every day Renren took the child to the mother's house to play and beg for food. Speaking of food, Miss Jiang craved the meals Da Niang cooked with own hands. Especially the bone broth: a single mouthful warmed the whole body. Now Da Niang no longer slaughtered hogs, and the work had been handed to Big Sister Taohua. Don't be fooled by Big Sister Taohua's beauty and willowy figure. Taohua had now become the market's new "female yaksha," famously fierce. At the slightest displeasure, Taohua could chase someone through three streets with a hog knife. On the opening day of Miss Jiang's clinic, someone brought gifts to propose marriage. The match had been arranged by Young Master Lin, newly passed as a Juren, who had asked a matchmaker to speak on his behalf. Coming along with them was none other than Miss Jiang's shameless father. In the turmoil years ago, the father had abandoned the aging mother and the small child and fled alone from the County Commandant's residence, hiding here and there. When the storm had passed and the father crept back, Miss Jiang's birth mother and younger brother had become only two skeletons. The father was now penniless, with no talent beyond a few sour lines of verse. With age, a bloated frame, and the handsome looks of former years entirely gone, no wealthy family cared to take on such a live-in son-in-law on looks alone. In the end the father had no choice but to go begging. Now, hearing of Miss Jiang's glittering fame, the father promptly clung on shamelessly. "Child! Your father has lived in such misery!" The father rushed before Miss Jiang, weeping and pouring out woes. No matter the pleading, no one paid any attention. In the end the father wiped away tears and said: "Fortunately you have made a name for yourself, and from now on your father will benefit too." "Oh, by the way, Juren Lin is very good. All these years he has kept waiting for you. You didn't save his mother back then, and still he holds no grudge. It is your good fortune! Agree quickly, and let your father go celebrate with the son-in-law..." Miss Jiang gently twirled a silver needle in hand and curled a lip at the father. When the father had finished speaking, Miss Jiang took a sheet of paper from the chest and waved it before the father's face. It was the document severing the father-daughter relationship. "Walk slowly. I won't see you out!" In truth, Miss Jiang had nothing left to say to the father. In childhood, Miss Jiang had loved and revered the father, later turning to hatred and blame, the heart so full of bitterness that opening the mouth would bring tears... But in the end, all those emotions had dissolved into mist and smoke. The father still refused to give up and showed up several more times. But the father knew full well that Miss Jiang was no gentle soul, and that in desperate straits Miss Jiang could kill a father without hesitation. After being chased off twice by household servants without mercy, nearly breaking the old bones, the father finally awoke to the fact that nothing could be gained from Miss Jiang. On the surface Miss Jiang looked gentle, yet the heart had actually hardened to iron and stone. The one person in this world who was truly kind and soft-hearted was only one. That was the woman with the fierce looks, who did not resemble a woman at all. Miss Jiang's mother, Jiang Dasao. That wretched father went before Miss Jiang's mother, tears streaming, begging to be allowed to return and live in peace, vowing never again to do wrong. By now Da Niang wore silk and brocade, with four maids attending and an occasional yawn from drowsiness. Having heard advice that the children had all made good, Da Niang had even invited a nanny to teach etiquette, and every day tried to learn how to become a kindly, benevolent old madam. But at the first honeyed words of the former husband, Da Niang flung every rule learned into the corner of the kitchen. Da Niang bounced up from the chair and drew, in one motion, the hog knife that had been hidden under the chair since who knew when. "Why won't you die already, you mad dog, and still dare come here to make trouble! I see your eyes are covered over with pig fat and your heart is smeared with hog dung. Let's see if I don't chop you into a hundred pieces today!" With that, Da Niang took the knife and chased the man at a dead run through the streets. Da Niang's bearing was nimble, commanding, and fierce beyond measure.
