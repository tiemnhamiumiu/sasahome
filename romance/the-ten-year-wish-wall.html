I put together a few more practice test sets, I'll send them over to you later." I knew I would be okay, but what about him? Before I could ask anything else, my consciousness began to blur. All I could do was stare at Blake standing in the middle of the schoolyard. Dressed in his blue and white track jacket, he smiled and waved at me, his figure drifting farther and farther away until it faded completely... When I woke up in the coffee shop, night had fallen. A light blanket was carefully draped over me. It was the barista's thoughtful gesture. I softly thanked her. "Ma'am, it's late. If you don't mind, you can stay in one of our guest rooms upstairs tonight." "That would be wonderful. Thank you so much." I was never a confident driver, and driving at night was dangerous. Besides, I really didn't want to go back to Riverdale right now. I followed her upstairs. On the way up, I couldn't hold back my question. "The customer named Blake who wrote all those notes downstairs... do you remember him?" "Blake? He's my cousin. He's actually the one who opened this coffee shop." As she spoke, she opened a door and turned on the light switch. "This used to be his bedroom. I clean it regularly, so it's very tidy. Please make yourself at home." I stood frozen for a moment before asking, "Where is he now?" "He's no longer with us," she said, her voice dropping with sorrow. "What do you mean..." The dark premonition in my heart finally turned into reality. "He passed away ten years ago. Back in 2012, he was fatally stabbed by a drunk man." My feet rooted to the spot, my entire body going cold as I looked around the room. Jay Chou posters were still taped to the walls, stacks of study materials were neatly organized on the desk alongside a basketball. This was Blake's private space. I walked quietly toward the desk. A thick book lay open on the surface. Glancing at the cover, the words "Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China" caught my eye. A horrifying thought suddenly flashed through my mind. Stunned, I looked closely at the open page. A few lines of law had been carefully underlined in red ink: "Article 233 of the Criminal Law: Whoever negligently causes the death of another shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not less than three years but not more than seven years; if the circumstances are relatively minor, he shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not more than three years. Article 234: Whoever intentionally inflicts bodily injury upon another shall be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of not more than three years, criminal detention, or public surveillance..." In that instant, I finally understood what he meant by not being able to keep his promise... My father hadn't accidentally killed someone during a fight back then. Blake had deliberately used himself as bait. He had intentionally provoked my violent father, pushing him to strike so that he could be put behind bars, freeing me from that endless nightmare... He had sacrificed his own life just to save me. Blake, why were you so foolish? Tears burst from my eyes, falling uncontrollably. Ignoring the barista's confused look, I sprinted downstairs like crazy and tore off the very last note remaining on the wall... The note contained only three words: "Goodbye, Summer." Traveling back ten years, I stood at the school gate clutching my backpack and checking my watch. It was that exact day. The day he wrote "Goodbye, Summer." Ignoring the loud ringing of the school bell, I rushed to borrow the custodian's phone to call the police, then sprinted toward home with everything I had. The bus only came every half hour, and I couldn't afford to wait. I ran with all my strength through the streets. Faster, Summer, run faster! The wind roared in my ears. Terror and panic surged within my chest. I was terrified that by the time I arrived, it would already be too late and Blake would be lying in a pool of blood. Thankfully, heaven showed mercy this time. When I burst into the familiar alley outside my building, Blake and my father were locked in a violent struggle. To my horror, the sharp blade in my father's hand was plunging straight toward Blake. "Blake, no! We still have to go to Beijing together!" I screamed with every ounce of strength I had left. Blake turned his head to look at me, a gentle smile appearing on his face. In the next second, my father raised the knife high into the air. At that exact moment, police sirens blared deafeningly from the entrance of the alley. I tried to rush forward to shield him, but my body collapsed from exhaustion. Frozen and unable to move, my consciousness slowly faded away... Perhaps out of fear that waking up would force me to face the bitter reality that I couldn't change Blake's fate, or perhaps because I was too terrified of losing him a second time, I didn't wake up in the coffee shop right away. Instead, I fell into a very long dream. It felt like Andersen's "The Little Mermaid"—both poetic and cruel. In the dream, Blake sacrificed his life to put my abusive father in prison, rescuing me from my dark nightmare, helping me pass the college entrance exam, and allowing me to enter Wuhan University as I had dreamed. Yet I had known nothing about his silent sacrifice. Instead, I spent my college years feeling deeply grateful to Ethan, who had stayed by my side. I believed his presence had healed my wounds, thinking he was the one who saved my life. I was just like the foolish prince in the fairytale, completely unaware that the little mermaid had quietly saved his life. Instead, I gave my gratitude to the stranger who happened to be there when I woke up, wanting to spend the rest of my life repaying him. Poor little mermaid, she gave up her beautiful voice for human legs, taking steps that felt like walking on sharp knives. She knew that if she couldn't win the prince's love and he married someone else, she would turn into sea foam. Yet she never regretted choosing to save the person she loved. Foolish, just like Blake. He had been so brilliant, not only excelling academically but also growing up in a warm, loving family. His future should have been bright and limitless. Yet seventeen-year-old Blake chose to trade his life just so seventeen-year-old Summer could pass her exams and move on. "Summer... Summer..." Through the haze, I heard a voice softly calling my name. Gradually pulling myself out of the long dream, an overwhelming sense of loss filled my heart. I thought I would only ever see Blake in my dreams again, yet the moment I opened my eyes, his figure was standing right in front of me. He stood beside me, looking down with a gentle smile. Overjoyed and stunned, I sat up instantly. I stared at him in utter disbelief, reaching up to pinch my own cheek as hard as I could. A sharp sting radiated across my cheek. This wasn't a dream! He pulled my hand away with a pained look, revealing a long scar running across his left arm. He spoke softly, "It's not a dream. I'm back. You saved me, Summer." Unable to hold back my feelings for another second, I threw my arms around him, burying my face in his chest. "Blake, I like you! Just like how you like me, I like you so much!" I looked up at him, finally answering the question left on seventeen-year-old Blake's sticky note from all those years ago. "I like you too," he whispered, looking down at me with a deep, steady voice. In 2012, a drunk man named Mr. Summer inflicted severe injuries to the left arm of a high school student named Blake with a knife. Because Blake and his family refused to settle, the court sentenced Mr. Summer to one year in prison. Seventeen-year-old Blake had used everything he had to save seventeen-year-old Summer. And twenty-seven-year-old Summer crossed through time and space to save seventeen-year-old Blake. "Goodbye, Summer." It was never a permanent farewell, but a promise to always stay by my side... Until we meet again.
